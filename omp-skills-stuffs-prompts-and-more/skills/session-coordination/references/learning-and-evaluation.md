# Maintain useful knowledge and evaluate changes

Read when a verified non-obvious finding has material future value, when current guidance is displaced, or when evaluating a proposed harness improvement. Routine successful edits need no retrospective. A first severe failure can justify durable learning without waiting for recurrence.

## Choose and maintain the narrowest home

Inspect relevant existing guidance before adding another claim. Prefer a regression test, type constraint, focused explanation of a hidden constraint, decision record, repository guide, or skill according to what prevents recurrence. Keep one canonical home where possible. An exact duplicate and a similar topic are different; preserve unique applicability and rationale.

Retain owner, applicability, source/version, failure/fix evidence, and invalidation or retirement condition. Classify claims as current, partly displaced, superseded, or unresolved. Preserve the reason an older decision once applied and the condition under which it might matter again; repair current inbound links. Keep immutable history and recoverable old run inputs intact. Age does not establish falsity, and unavailable verification does not establish disproof.

Separate current implementation facts from governing constraints. Code that violates a still-valid rule may be defective; do not weaken the rule merely to match it. Promotion and refresh are scoped maintenance actions, not automatic permission to rewrite active prompts or frozen run sources.

## Evaluate the decision, including its evaluator

Use the existing E1 comparison for uncertain harness changes. Freeze starting inputs, acceptance, and fault cases; compare the baseline with a focused addition before attributing a combined result. Preserve information-time boundaries. Keep tuning and final evaluation cases separate: inspecting or tuning against a holdout consumes its holdout status. Count attempted variants and related trial/source families, not only the winning run or nominally separate reviewer votes.

If an emulator or harness judges the policy, first calibrate its relevant behavior with fixed-action replay against the reference. Distinguish mock, recording, simulator/emulator, and actual-provider evidence; state which claims they establish. A passing substitute cannot prove live integration. Use independently determined expected results, meaningful invalid controls, and separate policy cases after calibration.

Compare accepted integrated outcomes and whole costs: inspection/setup, model/tool work, retries and failed attempts, review/integration, elapsed time, maintenance, and human correction. Include decision-relevant false completion, missed consumers, false acceptance by verifiers, scope expansion, and recovery outcomes. Expand cases only when uncertainty or a wider decision warrants it. Do not infer an application speedup from a microbenchmark or a general gain from one favorable run.

This reference extends E1; it neither runs a pilot nor replaces F3's actual parent/child loading and retained-source recovery proof. Use existing evaluation records rather than a new platform or universal numeric gate.
